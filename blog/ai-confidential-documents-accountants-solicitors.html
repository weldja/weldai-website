Can You Use AI with Confidential Client Documents? A Guide for UK Accountants and Solicitors

By James Weld May 2026, updated November 2026 7 min read
JW
James Weld
Weld AI · I install private, on-premise AI for UK small businesses

AI document tools are useful. But for accountants, solicitors, HR consultancies and anyone else handling confidential client information, the enthusiasm stops at an obvious question:

If I use AI on my documents, where exactly does my data go?

It is the right question, and the honest answer is that it depends entirely on how the tool is built. This guide sets out the three ways these tools work, in plain English, and what to look for if you are serious about client confidentiality.

Three ways AI document tools are built

1. Cloud AI: your documents are uploaded

ChatGPT file uploads, Google NotebookLM and most online AI assistants need your documents uploaded to their servers. Microsoft 365 Copilot works on files you have already moved into Microsoft's cloud. Either way, your files are processed and stored on servers you do not own or control.

These services are convenient and often well designed. But for a professional services firm, moving client documents outside your own systems raises immediate questions about confidentiality, data protection and professional conduct rules.

2. Partly local: your documents stay, but excerpts leave

Some tools sold as "private" or "self-hosted" keep your documents and the search index on your own computer. But to write each answer they send your question, and the passages they found, to a cloud AI model.

This is a real improvement on uploading everything. It is still a transfer of client information to a third party, usually outside the UK, every time someone asks a question. You need a data processing agreement, and you need to be comfortable with the provider's retention and training terms.

3. Fully on-premise: nothing leaves

The AI model itself runs on a computer in your office, alongside your documents and the search index. Nothing is sent anywhere. The system carries on working if you unplug the internet.

This is how Weld AI works. For a regulated firm, "only short excerpts are sent" is still one question too many.

The specific risks for regulated professionals

Solicitors

Solicitors must keep client information confidential and think carefully before sharing it with third parties. Uploading client documents to a cloud AI platform is sharing with a third party, and firms remain responsible for client data whatever tools they use.

For routine internal knowledge such as procedures, precedents and standard clauses, cloud AI is likely to be low risk. For client-specific files, the calculation changes significantly.

Accountants

Accountancy practices hold large volumes of personal data: tax returns, payroll records, management accounts. Under UK GDPR, passing this to a cloud AI provider needs a lawful basis and normally a data processing agreement with the provider.

Most major providers offer one, but read it carefully. Terms on retention, location and model training vary between products and between plans of the same product.

HR consultancies

HR files hold some of the most sensitive personal data a business has: disciplinary records, medical information, salaries. Putting them through a cloud AI tool without a proper assessment could create real exposure.

Important: this guide is general information, not legal advice. If you handle regulated client data, speak to your compliance officer or data protection lead before deploying any AI tool, including an on-premise one.

Questions to ask before using any AI document tool

  • When someone asks a question, does any text leave our building?
  • If so, who receives it, in which country, and how long do they keep it?
  • Do their terms rule out training on our data, on the plan we would actually buy?
  • Does it keep working with the internet disconnected? (The simplest test of an "on-premise" claim.)
  • Can we control which staff can search which documents?
  • Does every answer show its source, so staff can check it?
  • What happens to our data if we stop using the product?

How to verify an on-premise claim

"On-premise" and "private" are used loosely, so test them.

  • Unplug the network cable. Ask a question. A fully on-premise system answers as normal.
  • Ask your IT person to block the computer at the firewall. Nothing should break.
  • Ask where the AI model runs. If you are asked to create an account or an API key with an AI company, the model is in the cloud.

Weld AI passes all three. The AI model is installed on your PC with the software, and every release is tested to confirm it cannot reach the internet.

On-premise does not remove every obligation

Keeping data in the building removes the third-party problem. It does not remove your responsibilities inside the building.

  • Access. An AI assistant makes documents easier to find, including for people who should not find them. Use accounts and groups so that HR files stay with HR and matter files stay with the right team.
  • Accuracy. AI can be wrong. Answers should show their source, and staff should check anything they rely on.
  • The computer itself. It now holds a searchable index of your documents. Keep it secure, patched and backed up like any other machine holding client data.

A sensible way to start

Begin with internal knowledge: procedures, policies, handbooks, precedents and templates. Your team gets the benefit immediately and gets used to checking sources. Then extend to client files once your compliance lead is happy with the access controls.

The bottom line

AI document search is not incompatible with client confidentiality, but the architecture matters enormously. A tool that uploads your documents, a tool that sends excerpts, and a tool that sends nothing are three different propositions, even when all three are described as "secure".

For a firm whose first duty is confidentiality, the simplest position to explain to a client or a regulator is this: the AI runs in our office, and nothing leaves it.
